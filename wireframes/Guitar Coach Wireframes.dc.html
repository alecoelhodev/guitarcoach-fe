2gCross-platform notesWhat one React Native codebase can share
Shared without change
Task card, routine card, session card, recording row, badges, chips, segmented control, checklist row, stepper, inline validation, toast, network-error panel, skeletons, and the AI plan-preview card. These are the same components at the same sizes — web just places more of them per row.
One component, two arrangements
The routine task row: stacked with tap-to-expand on mobile, one line with controls always visible on web. The recording row: two stacked lines on mobile, one row with an inline scrubber on web. Both are a single component branching on available width, not two implementations.
Navigation is the real fork
Bottom tabs with a raised central action on mobile; a left rail on web at 768px and up, where History and AI Coach get their own entries. Same routes, same order, different container — worth isolating behind one navigation component so screens never know which shell they are in.
Sheets become panels or dialogs
Every bottom sheet in turn 1 has a web counterpart: the routine picker becomes an inline panel, confirmations become centred dialogs, and the Practice choice sheet becomes a small popover under the rail's Practice button. Keep the content identical and let the presenter differ.
Web-only affordances
Drag-and-drop file upload, hover states on rows, keyboard shortcuts in the session screen, deep-linkable list/detail URLs, and the browser back button. None of these have mobile equivalents, so none of them can be the only way to do anything.
Layout rules that carry both ways
One content column capped at 560px on mobile and tablet; two panes above 1024px for Library, Routines and History; conversation capped at 640px; the Active Session screen never splits. 44px minimum targets stay on web — they cost nothing with a mouse and matter on touchscreen laptops and tablets.

Nothing is written until Save. The draft card carries a “Not saved” badge for the whole time it is on screen.
Why the two modes exist, and yes — both are supported. They are two different endpoints with two different contracts. Draft & Review returns a plan awaiting confirmation and nothing else happens; the routine is only created when the user confirms, and declining cancels it outright. It is the mode for “I want a say in the plan.” Instant Create is the agent path: it reads recent practice and creates the routine in the same request, so there is nothing to confirm. Exposing them as one ambiguous button would hide the only thing the user needs to know — whether a routine is about to appear in their list.
Drafts expire after 15 minutes. The pending plan is held server-side for that long and tied to the person who asked for it; past that, confirming returns nothing and the draft must be regenerated — hence the expired state opposite.

Biggest type on the screen is the clock; the primary action is a 52px target reachable with a thumb. Nothing is written to the server mid-session — the timer and ticks are local until Finish.
The clock is a local stopwatch, not a saved field. A session stores only a per-task minute count and a completed flag, and both are optional — there is no total-elapsed value on a session. So the stopwatch exists to help the guitarist pace themselves; what actually gets saved is the whole-minute number on each task, which is why the stepper is minutes and why every total elsewhere in the app is a sum of those numbers. If someone never touches the stepper, the task saves with no duration and History shows the completion count without minutes — the interface must read correctly with the minutes missing.

1hAdaptation notesWider phones and responsive web
Wider phones (up to ~430pt)
Everything above is a single 320pt column that grows with the viewport; cards stay full-width and gain internal padding rather than a second column. The bottom nav keeps its fixed 58px central action and distributes the four tabs evenly. Line lengths on task descriptions cap at about 60 characters, so extra width goes to margins.
Tablet and small web (600–1024px)
Content column caps at 560px and centres. Library, Routines and History switch to a two-column card grid. Bottom sheets become centred modals of the same width. The bottom nav moves to a left rail at 768px and up, with Practice as the first, emphasised item.
Wide web (1024px+)
Two panes: list on the left, detail on the right — Library beside Task Details, History beside Session Details, Routines beside the Builder. Active Practice Session stays single-column and centred, since it is used at arm's length. AI Coach becomes a centred 640px conversation with the plan preview inline.
Touch and reach
Every tap target is at least 44×44px, including the show/hide password toggle, the duration steppers and the drag handles. Primary actions sit in the lower half of the screen; destructive actions are always last in their group and never adjacent to a confirm.
Reordering without dragging
The drag handle is the fast path, but every routine task also exposes Move up / Move down once expanded, so reordering works with assistive tech and with one hand on a guitar neck.
What is deliberately absent
No task creation or editing (admin-only), no session editing after finishing, no analytics beyond weekly minutes and session count, no waveform or scoring on recordings, and no social, premium or gamified surfaces.
